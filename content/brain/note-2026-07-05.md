There's a counterintuitive truth I've learned across hundreds of vendor negotiations: the procurement teams that extract the most value aren't the ones who squeeze hardest on price.

When you manage 83+ stations, you're constantly negotiating — ground handling agreements, equipment leases, service contracts. Early in my career, I thought winning meant getting the lowest number on paper. Now I know that's often a loss in disguise.

Here's what actually works: radical clarity on what you need, flexibility on how you get it.

Last year we renegotiated a major equipment contract. Instead of leading with price pressure, we led with operational specifications — exactly what uptime we required, exactly what response windows we needed, exactly what our growth trajectory looked like. We gave them certainty about our volume and timeline. In exchange, we got preferential rates without asking for them.

The paradox: vendors give better deals to customers who reduce their risk, not to customers who create adversarial dynamics. A supplier who knows exactly what you need, when you need it, and that you'll actually follow through? They'll sharpen their pencil voluntarily.

This applies beyond procurement. I see the same pattern in hiring, in cross-functional partnerships, in getting buy-in from regional teams. Clarity about your constraints and goals — genuine clarity, not negotiating posture — creates space for creative solutions.

The amateur move is opacity: hide your budget, obscure your timeline, keep them guessing. The professional move is selective transparency: share what helps them help you, protect what genuinely needs protecting.

Most negotiations fail not because someone won't budge on price, but because neither side actually understands what the other needs to say yes.

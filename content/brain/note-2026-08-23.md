I've sat through hundreds of vendor demonstrations. Ground handling systems, rostering software, baggage reconciliation platforms, aircraft turnaround tools. Every single one runs flawlessly in the demo environment.

The demo airport has perfect weather. The demo workforce shows up fully staffed. The demo flights arrive on schedule. The demo data is clean, complete, consistent.

Then you deploy it at a station where summer temperatures hit 48°C, where three handlers called in sick, where the inbound aircraft is 47 minutes late and you have a 35-minute turnaround window that cannot slip.

This is where I've learned to redirect every vendor conversation: "Show me the failure mode."

Not what happens when everything works. What happens when the system loses connectivity mid-operation? When the data feed from the airline is malformed? When the user makes an input error under pressure? When you need to override the algorithm because the algorithm doesn't know that Stand 14 has a fuel truck blocking access?

The answers tell you everything. Vendors who've actually deployed in operational environments understand the question immediately. They've lived it. They can show you the manual fallback, the offline capability, the audit trail that protects your team when something goes wrong.

Vendors who've only sold to IT departments get uncomfortable. They start talking about uptime percentages and SLAs.

Uptime percentages don't push back a departure. My station manager does.

When I evaluate any system now — whether I'm procuring it for 83 stations or building it myself for personal automation — I start from the assumption that it will fail. The question isn't whether. It's how gracefully, how visibly, and how quickly we recover.

The demo is marketing. The failure mode is the product.

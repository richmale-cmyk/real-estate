I've watched this pattern destroy operational performance more times than I can count: procurement scores the lowest price on a ground handling contract, celebrates the savings, then operations spends the next three years drowning in delays, damage claims, and customer complaints that cost five times what we "saved."

The fundamental problem is measurement asymmetry. Procurement can quantify what they saved. Operations struggles to quantify what the cheap decision cost — the knock-on delays, the crews timing out, the misconnects, the brand damage from a wheelchair passenger left on the jetbridge.

Here's what I've learned works:

**Build the failure cost into the evaluation model before you tender.** Not after. I now require historical performance data weighted at 40% of the scoring matrix. A handler with three on-time performance points below the leader needs to bid 15% cheaper just to break even in my model. Most can't.

**Make procurement own a slice of operational KPIs.** When the sourcing team's bonus partially depends on departure punctuality at stations they contracted, their vendor conversations change overnight.

**Price the switching cost honestly.** Changing ground handlers at a major station isn't a contract signature — it's 6-8 months of chaos. The "savings" from switching to save 4% rarely survives contact with reality.

The vendors know this game. The good ones price fairly and deliver. The desperate ones lowball to win, then nickel-and-dime you on extras or simply understaff until you're trapped.

My rule now: I'd rather pay 10% more for a handler I've watched perform than save 10% gambling on one I haven't. The operational margin we protect is worth more than the procurement margin we chase.

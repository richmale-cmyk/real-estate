There's a pattern I've watched play out across dozens of contract renewals: the incumbent wins not because they're best, but because switching costs feel unknowable.

Ground handling contracts are the perfect example. You've got a provider who knows your operation, has relationships with your station managers, understands your SLAs. They're adequate. Maybe even good. So when renewal comes, the internal conversation becomes about risk mitigation rather than value optimisation.

"Do we really want to transition during peak season?"
"They know our quirks."
"The new provider might be cheaper on paper, but what about the learning curve?"

These aren't bad questions. They're incomplete ones.

What I've learned to do: treat incumbent renewals with *more* rigour than new vendor selection, not less. The incumbent has had years to embed their inefficiencies into your operation. You've adapted to their limitations without noticing. Your teams have built workarounds that mask their gaps.

The real question isn't "what's the switching cost?" It's "what's the cost of not knowing what better looks like?"

I run a parallel evaluation now — even when I'm 80% certain we'll renew. Not to waste anyone's time, but because the process itself reveals things. New vendors ask different questions. They challenge assumptions we stopped examining. Sometimes they expose that the incumbent's pricing has drifted 15% above market without us noticing.

The other thing: incumbents negotiate differently when they know you've done the work. The dynamic shifts from "what will you accept?" to "what do we need to offer to keep this?"

Comfort is expensive. Not always in money — sometimes in capability you never knew you were missing.

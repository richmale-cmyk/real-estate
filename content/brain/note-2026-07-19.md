I've sat through hundreds of vendor demos. Ground handling systems, baggage reconciliation platforms, crew management tools, AI copilots for ops control. The pattern is always the same: vendor presents, we nod politely, ask a few questions, then "align internally" before the next meeting.

This is backwards.

The demo is not a presentation. It's the first negotiation session, and the vendor knows this even if you don't. Every feature they emphasise, every use case they skip, every question they deflect — it's all positioning. They're reading you, understanding your pain points, calibrating their pricing strategy.

So I started treating demos differently. Three shifts:

**Ask operational questions, not feature questions.** Not "does it do X?" but "what happens when X fails at 3am and my duty manager has 40 minutes until the next departure?" Watch how they respond. Do they have an answer, or do they pivot to a roadmap promise?

**State constraints early, not late.** I'll mention budget limitations or integration requirements in the first meeting, not the third. This filters out vendors who were never going to work anyway and gives serious ones time to build a realistic proposal. Saves everyone time.

**Bring the end user.** My station managers or handling supervisors sit in key demos. Not because I need their technical input — I need the vendor to see who actually has to live with their product at 5am in Karachi or Tbilisi. It changes the conversation.

The best procurement outcomes I've achieved came from vendors who understood our operation before the negotiation phase even started. That understanding gets built — or destroyed — in the demo.

Treat it accordingly.

Every year, I watch procurement teams obsess over new contracts while auto-renewing existing ones. The logic seems sound: focus energy on the unknown, trust the proven. But I've learned this instinct costs more than most people realise.

At 83+ stations, we have hundreds of vendor relationships. Ground handling, cleaning, catering, security, transport. When I started auditing renewals properly, I found something uncomfortable: our longest-standing vendors often had the worst value-to-cost ratios. Not because they were bad — because the relationship had calcified.

Here's what happens. Year one, you negotiate hard. Both sides are sharp. Year two, minor uplift, nobody questions it. Year five, you're paying 15-20% above market because everyone's comfortable. The vendor knows you won't switch — the switching cost feels too high. Your team knows leadership won't question a renewal — it's not a new spend.

This is comfortable procurement. It's expensive.

I've started treating every renewal over a certain threshold as a new negotiation. Not adversarial — the relationship matters — but genuinely questioning the value exchange. What's changed? What should cost less now? What are we paying for that we no longer use?

The pushback I get: "We'll damage the relationship." But I've found the opposite. Good vendors respect rigour. They know they've gotten comfortable too. The ones who get defensive when you ask questions are usually the ones who've been coasting.

The harder truth: comfortable procurement often reflects comfortable leadership. If my team isn't questioning established relationships, it's because I haven't made it safe — or expected — for them to do so.

Every renewal is a decision. Treat it like one.

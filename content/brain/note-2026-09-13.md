Most people think negotiation is about what happens in the room. The back-and-forth. The tactics. The pressure.

I've learned it's almost entirely about what happens before anyone sits down.

In ground handling contracts, by the time we're at the table discussing rates per turn, I've already shaped the outcome through months of preparation. Benchmarking data from comparable stations. Operational audits that documented exactly where the incumbent failed on SLAs. Alternative vendor relationships cultivated not as bluffs, but as genuine options.

The meeting itself becomes a formality — a place to confirm what both parties already know is reasonable.

Three things I always establish before a significant negotiation:

**My BATNA is real, not theoretical.** If I'm negotiating with a fuel supplier and my alternative is a different supplier I've never actually spoken to, that's not a BATNA. That's a hope. I talk to the alternative. I get their proposal in writing. Sometimes I run a trial.

**I understand their constraints, not just their position.** A handling agent saying they can't reduce rates might be protecting margin, or they might be underwater on labour costs they can't control. The response to each is completely different.

**The relationship equity is already deposited.** You can't withdraw trust you haven't banked. The vendors who get flexibility from me during disruptions are the ones who give me flexibility on renewals. This isn't transactional — it's how sustainable partnerships work.

The worst negotiations I've been part of all shared one trait: someone showed up thinking they'd figure it out in the room.

By then, you're just reacting to whoever prepared better.
